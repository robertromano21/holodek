# Additive Stonework Design

## Principle

Stonework should help explain how a building was made, who used it, and what matters inside it. Ornament belongs on actual architectural parts: floors, door jambs, lintels, capitals, cornices, balustrades, screens, altars and tombs. Richer architecture does not require adding more obstacles or pillars.

Keep the existing fresco compositions, fourteen carving patterns, sculptures, altar forms and mechanical puzzle kits. New families supplement them. The research below is a reference for construction principles, not a request to reproduce museum objects, import real-world religions or copy the user's watermarked photograph.

## Construction Layers

1. Keep the load-bearing architectural module and its collision contract authoritative.
2. Choose an ornamental role: threshold, border, portal surround, capital, screen, frieze or focal narrative relief.
3. Select a coherent building tradition, material and scale before choosing motifs. A complex can preserve one visual language across rooms, with later repairs introducing limited hybrids.
4. Assemble repeatable middles with proper endpoints, corners and junctions. Random seed choices should change composition within a grammar, not randomize every adjacent piece.
5. Add campaign patronage and stable lore metadata separately from combat item properties. A decorative image does not become a quest event or a new interaction merely because it depicts one.
6. Reserve navigable clearances, sightlines and legible lighting. Do not conceal mandatory clues or cover frescoes with candle fixtures.

Fibonacci proportions, rotational symmetries and curve grammars can inform layout, but no numerical sequence alone guarantees good architecture. Useful variation comes from changing the role, arrangement, depth, motif hierarchy, material, wear and building history together.

## Implemented In This Pass

- Ordinary new indoor buildings retain their original four-to-six authored artworks and may gain three matching carved wall bands plus a level, reachable entrance paving panel. Bands use consistent dado, skirting or cornice heights, and proportioned center/end modules rather than one stretched image. These are additional to the authored art allowance and do not alter floor collision.
- Stonework takes its stone color from the room's actual wall or floor texture, excluding dark mortar and bright decal extremes. Carved highlights and recesses use lighter/darker shades of that same color, not an unrelated gold palette. Saved bands are recolored and assembled only in the renderer; their stored bounds and readable metadata are not rewritten.
- Existing carving patterns now have building-tradition-specific selection pools; all fourteen remain available to authored designs.
- Mosaics share canonical figure anatomy and costume metadata, but have independent composition templates and pose defaults. Byzantine pieces retain gold-ground colors, staggered grout and distinct tessera facets. They are bounded code-generated images, not imported paintings.
- Obelisks are supported as readable inscription fixtures, with persistent text and vertical emblem registers on all four faces.
- Existing saved mural lamps are relocated onto flanking backing masonry only at render time. Light sampling avoids wall-grid corners, and local illumination preserves painted pigments.
- Statue receivers can use the refined renderer without changing a saved mechanism ID. Finer geometry and local cavity shading help distinguish faces, hands and drapery. These are stylized voxel sculptures, not finished classical realism.

It subtracts a shallow layer of occupied stone voxels from the existing solid volume and retains backing, rims, shafts and bearing edges. Recesses require solid backing and remove at most 2.9% of occupied voxels. The normal voxel mesher emits the exposed recess walls and normals; no new texture, painted skin or standalone overlay supplies the detail. Invalid/incompatible descriptors leave the original grid intact.

Unsupported timber posts/trusses, metal, natural rocks, floors, mechanisms and readable inscriptions are not indiscriminately carved. Thin squinches remain unchanged where there is insufficient backing. Column shafts and capitals retain their original fluted silhouettes. Successful new roof construction tags all compatible supports and structural parts; it reuses one variant per source tile, preserves unrelated cell identifiers, and leaves rejected roofs atomic. The full surface repair places positive faces on the actual occupied-voxel boundary, rather than one voxel inward. ### Remaining Volume Work

Prioritize stone roundel/niche adapters, supported portal relief, fitted capital and spandrel adapters. These remain proposed additions, not implemented capabilities; corbel and coffer adapters are now implemented. True pierced screens should follow a separate visibility, lighting and collision design.

Openwork needs real holes and a separate rendering/lighting contract: painting a lattice on an opaque wall does not produce transparent openings or accurate patterned shadows. Screen collision should retain the protective barrier while visual apertures and light transmission remain distinct. The current coarse cell shadow grid cannot represent every pierced detail.

Deep sculptures and balcony surrounds need actual supported volumes; shallow engraved borders and distant ornament can use the existing bounded height-map system. Cache reusable geometry and compiled art, preserve the existing mesh-index budget, and validate the aggregate cost in a crowded room.

Persistent inscriptions should be exactly readable. Decorative marks may suggest a history, but puzzle clues must still be backed by implemented controls, verified solutions and an accessible reference diagram.
